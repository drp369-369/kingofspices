# Problem Identification and Customer Validation

## Evidence status

No survey responses, interview notes, observation log or sourced market evidence was found in the repository. The website's testimonials are demonstration content, not validated research. The problem framing below is a **hypothesis to validate**, not a finding about a measured market.

## Problem hypothesis

Independent restaurants may lose direct-order revenue and operational visibility when customer ordering, repeat visits, event inquiries and kitchen/inventory tracking depend on disconnected or manual processes. Customers may find ordering, understanding delivery costs, tracking order progress and requesting event catering inconvenient. This prototype explores whether one direct web experience plus a lightweight operations view can reduce those frictions.

## Why it could matter

If the hypothesis holds for the selected restaurant, fragmented intake could create avoidable staff effort, missed inquiries, stock mistakes and weak repeat-customer relationships. The severity, frequency and financial impact are unknown until the team observes and interviews the intended users. Do not claim a percentage improvement or market size without collected evidence and a source.

## Initial target personas (hypotheses)

| Persona | Context | Jobs and likely pain points to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Local repeat customer | Orders from a nearby independent restaurant, usually by phone, walk-in or delivery platform | Wants reliable menu/price visibility, a simple reorder and an honest status; friction may include unclear fees or repeating details. |
| Restaurant owner/manager | Oversees orders, menu, staff and stock with limited time | Wants incoming orders in one queue, fewer missed requests and usable stock visibility; friction may include manual handoffs and inconsistent records. |
| Catering/event organizer | Plans a group event with budget and date constraints | Wants a quick estimate and clear follow-up; friction may include slow quote turnaround and uncertainty about package options. |

These are research prompts, not confirmed personas. Validate which segment has the strongest repeated problem before expanding scope.

## Existing alternatives to investigate

Phone/WhatsApp, walk-in ordering, printed menus, spreadsheets/notebooks, restaurant-owned sites, and third-party delivery/booking platforms. Their availability, cost and shortcomings vary by business and location; the project team must interview users about their actual alternatives rather than assume the limitations.

## Proposed solution and value proposition

An accessible direct restaurant web experience that combines menu and cart, transparent estimated charges, an order record, membership promotion, event inquiry estimate and a staff-facing local order/inventory demonstration. **Value hypothesis:** a restaurant could own a clearer direct customer journey and see its demo orders and stock changes in one place. This prototype does not yet prove operational savings or support production service.

## Validation plan (two weeks)

1. Recruit 5–8 restaurant customers and 3–5 restaurant operators from the intended local segment; confirm consent and avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.
2. Conduct 15–20 minute semi-structured interviews before showing the prototype. Ask about the last real order/event and current workflow.
3. Observe 3–5 users completing a representative task with the prototype; record completion, errors, time and verbatim feedback with permission.
4. Summarize dated notes by recurring theme; distinguish direct quotes, researcher interpretation and sample limitations.
5. Define a decision threshold before testing: continue a feature only if multiple target users report the same recent problem and the prototype materially helps in observed tasks. Team should set exact thresholds after recruitment feasibility is known.

### Customer interview questions

1. Tell me about the last time you ordered from / managed orders for a local restaurant.
2. How did the process start, and what steps took the most effort?
3. What went wrong the last time? How often does that happen?
4. How do you currently see menu availability, delivery fees and order progress?
5. How do you handle group catering or reservations today?
6. What tools or services do you use instead, and what do they cost in time or money?
7. Which part of the current process would you most want to change? Why?
8. What information would you need before trusting a restaurant's direct ordering site?
9. For an operator: how are orders accepted, status updated and stock reconciled today?
10. What would make you stop using a new tool?

Avoid leading with feature pitches. Ask for examples from recent behavior, not hypothetical willingness alone.

## Lightweight usability observation sheet

Record date, participant code (not name), persona, device, task, completion (yes/no), time, errors/help required, observed quote, severity and proposed change. Tasks: find a dish and total; place a synthetic order; find its status; change its kitchen status; request a catering estimate. Never enter real payment or sensitive personal data.

## Expected impact measures (to collect)

- Customer: task completion rate, time to order, checkout error rate, clarity rating for total and status.
- Operator: time to locate/update an order, stock adjustment errors, missed inquiry count in a real pilot.
- Business: direct-order share, repeat rate and inquiry-to-booking conversion only in a consented, instrumented pilot with an agreed baseline.

No baseline or result is available yet.
